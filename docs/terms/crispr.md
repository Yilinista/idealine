# CRISPR: where it came from

CRISPR began in the early 2000s as a name for a family of repeated DNA sequences found in bacterial and archaeal genomes, which researchers then showed to be part of an adaptive immune system against viruses and plasmids. Since 2013 it has mainly meant a programmable, RNA-guided toolkit, above all CRISPR-Cas9, used for genome editing, genetic screens, gene regulation, diagnostics and emerging therapies.

## Eras

- **2002–2006 · Mysterious prokaryotic repeats**: CRISPR named a family of clustered, regularly spaced repeat elements separated by unique spacers in prokaryotic genomes, whose function was still being worked out.
- **2007–2012 · Bacterial adaptive immune system**: CRISPR and its Cas genes were understood as an RNA-guided defense system that gives bacteria and archaea acquired immunity against phages and plasmids.
- **2013–2016 · Programmable genome-editing tool**: CRISPR-Cas9 was repurposed as an RNA-guided nuclease for editing genomes in human cells, animals, plants and microbes, for genome-wide screens and for control of gene expression.
- **2017–2026 · Diversified platform and therapies**: CRISPR became a broad technology platform spanning RNA-targeting Cas13, diagnostics based on Cas12 and Cas13, perturbation screens, crop engineering and clinical in vivo editing.

## Milestones

- **2002 · coined**: [Identification of genes that are associated with DNA repeats in prokaryotes](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2958.2002.02839.x). The authors proposed the name 'clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR)' for this family of repeats.
  - > To appreciate their characteri-stic structure, we will refer to this family as the clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR). (Ruud Jansen et al., 2002)
- **2005 · redefined**: [Clustered regularly interspaced short palindrome repeats (CRISPRs) have spacers of extrachromosomal origin](https://doi.org/10.1099/mic.0.28048-0). This work showed that the spacers come from foreign DNA, linking the repeats to phage and plasmid sequences.
  - > The remarkable repetitive elements called CRISPRs (clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats) consist of repeats interspaced with non-repetitive elements or 'spacers'. (Christine Pourcel et al., 2005)
  - > Numerous prokaryote genomes contain structures known as clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPRs), composed of 25-50 bp repeats separated by unique sequence spacers of similar length. (Alexander Bolotin et al., 2005)
- **2006 · redefined**: [A putative RNA-interference-based immune system in prokaryotes](https://doi.org/10.1186/1745-6150-1-7). It reinterpreted CRISPR-cas, previously linked to DNA repair, as a hypothesized RNAi-like immune system.
  - > BACKGROUND: All archaeal and many bacterial genomes contain Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindrome Repeats (CRISPR) and variable arrays of the CRISPR-associated (cas) genes that have been previously implicated in a novel form of DNA repair on the basis of comparative analysis of their protein product sequences. (Kira S. Makarova et al., 2006)
- **2007 · redefined**: [Phage Response to CRISPR-Encoded Resistance in Streptococcus thermophilus](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.01412-07). It linked CRISPR experimentally to acquired resistance against bacteriophages.
  - > Clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR) and their associated genes are linked to a mechanism of acquired resistance against bacteriophages. (Hélène Deveau et al., 2007)
- **2008 · redefined**: [Small CRISPR RNAs Guide Antiviral Defense in Prokaryotes](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1159689). It showed that small CRISPR RNAs guide antiviral defense, establishing the RNA-guided mechanism.
  - > Prokaryotes acquire virus resistance by integrating short fragments of viral nucleic acid into clusters of regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPRs). (Stan J. J. Brouns et al., 2008)
  - > Clustered, regularly interspaced, short palindromic repeat (CRISPR) loci confer sequence-directed immunity against phages. (Luciano A. Marraffini et al., 2008)
- **2012 · redefined**: [Cas9–crRNA ribonucleoprotein complex mediates specific DNA cleavage for adaptive immunity in bacteria](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1208507109). It characterized Cas9 as an RNA-guided DNA endonuclease, setting up its later use as a tool.
  - > Clustered, regularly interspaced, short palindromic repeats (CRISPR)/CRISPR-associated (Cas) systems provide adaptive immunity against viruses and plasmids in bacteria and archaea. (Giedrius Gasiūnas et al., 2012)
- **2013 · applied**: [Multiplex Genome Engineering Using CRISPR/Cas Systems](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1231143). It moved the CRISPR/Cas system into mammalian genome engineering and became the most-cited paper in the evidence.
  - > The type II prokaryotic CRISPR (clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats)/Cas adaptive immune system has been shown to facilitate RNA-guided site-specific DNA cleavage. (Le Cong et al., 2013)
  - > Bacteria and archaea have evolved adaptive immune defenses, termed clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR)/CRISPR-associated (Cas) systems, that use short RNA to direct degradation of foreign nucleic acids. (Prashant Mali et al., 2013)
- **2013 · applied**: [Repurposing CRISPR as an RNA-Guided Platform for Sequence-Specific Control of Gene Expression](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2013.02.022). It extended CRISPR beyond cutting DNA to regulating transcription.
  - > Repurposing CRISPR as an RNA-Guided Platform for Sequence-Specific Control of Gene Expression (Lei S. Qi et al., 2013)
  - > CRISPR-Mediated Modular RNA-Guided Regulation of Transcription in Eukaryotes (Luke A. Gilbert et al., 2013)
- **2013 · applied**: [Genome-Scale CRISPR-Cas9 Knockout Screening in Human Cells](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1247005). It established genome-wide CRISPR screens as a way to study gene function.
  - > The simplicity of programming the CRISPR (clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats)-associated nuclease Cas9 to modify specific genomic loci suggests a new way to interrogate gene function on a genome-wide scale. (Ophir Shalem et al., 2013)
  - > The bacterial clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR)-Cas9 system for genome editing has greatly expanded the toolbox for mammalian genetics, enabling the rapid generation of isogenic cell lines and mice with modified alleles. (Wang Tim et al., 2013)

- **2017 · applied**: [Nucleic acid detection with CRISPR-Cas13a/C2c2](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aam9321). It used the collateral cleavage activity of a CRISPR enzyme for diagnostics, opening the CRISPR-Dx field.
  - > The RNA-guided, RNA-targeting clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR) effector Cas13a (previously known as C2c2) exhibits a "collateral effect" of promiscuous ribonuclease activity upon target recognition. (Jonathan S. Gootenberg et al., 2017)

- **2021 · applied**: [CRISPR-Cas9 In Vivo Gene Editing for Transthyretin Amyloidosis](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2107454). It marked the translation of CRISPR editing into human in vivo therapy.
