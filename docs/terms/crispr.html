CRISPR

CRISPR began in the early 2000s as a name for a family of repeated DNA sequences found in bacterial and archaeal genomes, which researchers then showed to be part of an adaptive immune system against viruses and plasmids. Since 2013 it has mainly meant a programmable, RNA-guided toolkit, above all CRISPR-Cas9, used for genome editing, genetic screens, gene regulation, diagnostics and emerging therapies.
