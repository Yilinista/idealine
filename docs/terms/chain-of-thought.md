# chain-of-thought: where it came from

For most of the 20th century, 'chain of thought' was an everyday English phrase for a person's line of reasoning. In 2022 it was taken up to name a way of prompting large language models to write out intermediate reasoning steps. Today it mostly means the step-by-step reasoning traces that language models generate, including the 'long chain-of-thought' that reasoning models are trained to produce with reinforcement learning. These traces are also studied for efficiency, faithfulness and AI-safety monitoring.

## Eras

- **1953–2021 · Everyday idiom for reasoning**: A person's connected line of thought or argument, used loosely in prose across many fields.
- **2022–2022 · Prompting technique for LLMs**: A method of prompting large language models with step-by-step examples or instructions so that they generate intermediate reasoning steps.
- **2023–2023 · Reasoning traces, broadly applied**: CoT became a general name for model-generated reasoning: used for finetuning and distillation, extended to multimodal and robotic tasks, and scrutinized for faithfulness.
- **2024–2026 · Trained long CoT reasoning**: CoT increasingly names the extended reasoning that reasoning models are trained, often with reinforcement learning, to produce at test time; its length, cost and monitorability became central concerns.

  - > One of the most prominent stylistic means applied by Woolf is her ‘train of thought’, linking one reflection to another like wagons in a railway convoy or like loops in a chain (therefore also sometimes referred to as ‘chain of thought’ in dictionaries). (Ellen Johansson, 2006)
- **2022 · origin**: [Chain-Of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models](https://doi.org/10.52202/068431-1800). This is the earliest and most-cited use in the evidence defining a chain of thought as a series of intermediate reasoning steps generated by LLMs.
  - > We explore how generating a chain of thought -- a series of intermediate reasoning steps -- significantly improves the ability of large language models to perform complex reasoning. (Jason Wei et al., 2022)
  - > Providing these steps for prompting demonstrations is called chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting. (Zhuosheng Zhang et al., 2022)
- **2022 · popularized**: [Large Language Models Are Zero-Shot Reasoners](https://doi.org/10.52202/068431-1613). It extended CoT prompting to the zero-shot setting and framed it as a state-of-the-art technique, spreading its use.

- **2022 · redefined**: [Scaling Instruction-Finetuned Language Models](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2210.11416). CoT moved from being a prompt to being training data that models are finetuned on.
